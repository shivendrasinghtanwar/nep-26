# Nepal Entry Rules — Indian Vehicles & Travellers (2026 reference)

> Last compiled: 2026-05-04. Sourced from model knowledge through May 2025 + your supplied 2026 operating notes. **Re-verify before crossing.**

## 1. Identity & Visa

Indian nationals do **not** require a passport or visa to enter Nepal. Acceptable photo ID: passport, Voter ID, or Indian Government PAN/Aadhaar (Aadhaar alone is *not* preferred at hotels — pair it with Voter ID). Minors below 16 should travel with a parent and a school ID or birth-certificate copy.

## 2. Vehicle — Bhansar (Customs) Permit

Issued by Nepal Customs at the entry point (Sunauli, Birgunj, Kakarvitta etc.).

- Required for any private Indian vehicle entering Nepal.
- Daily fee, paid in INR or NPR, receipt-based.
- Indicative fee band (small/large car/SUV) — confirm at counter.
- Carry: e-RC (printout, self-attested), valid insurance, owner's photo ID, and — for a firm-registered vehicle — an authorization letter from the firm proprietor.
- Mahindra Thar Roxx (firm-registered) **must** carry the authorization letter explicitly stating "Personal Travel" usage to avoid being assessed under commercial-vehicle slabs.

## 3. Vehicle — Yatayat (Transport) Permit

Issued by Nepal Department of Transport Management at the same border post. Required alongside Bhansar.

- Daily fee, paid separately from Bhansar.
- Practical tip: pay for **2–3 buffer days beyond planned exit** to absorb any landslide / weather / mechanical delay in Mustang. Refunds are not common but extensions while inside Nepal are messy.

## 4. The 30-Day Cumulative Cap (2026)

For 2026, Nepal has tightened private Indian vehicle entry to a **30-day cumulative annual cap**. Days are counted by Bhansar/Yatayat permit duration, not by physical presence.

- Your 2026-05-09 → 2026-05-23 trip = ~15 days.
- Permits with 2–3 day buffer = 17–18 days against the cap.
- After this trip, ~12 days remain for any later 2026 entry.
- **Vehicle impoundment** is the stated penalty for overstay.
- *Action:* keep a phone-note running tally; photograph every Bhansar entry/exit stamp.

## 5. Customs on Personal Goods (NPR 100 rule + electronics)

- New unboxed goods imported as personal baggage are subject to assessment if total value exceeds the threshold (commonly cited as NPR 10,000 / per-item NPR 100 for small items — *confirm at counter*).
- Used personal electronics (laptops, phones, cameras) are **exempt** if you can show purchase invoices / office IDs proving prior ownership.
- For **two MacBook Pros**: carry both purchase invoices + business letterhead "issued for office use" note. Declare verbally; do not hide.
- New phones / cameras still in packaging will likely be assessed.

## 6. Driving in Nepal — Practical Rules

- Drive on the left (same as India).
- Speed limits: 40 km/h in towns, 60–80 km/h on highways, 30 km/h in mountain switchbacks.
- Headlights ON in mountain sections — local custom.
- Honking is more frequent than India in Pokhara — don't read it as aggressive.
- **Drinking & driving** — zero tolerance, immediate fine + possible vehicle hold.
- Police checks are routine on the Mugling–Pokhara road. Have permits + ID ready.

## 7. Fuel & ATMs

- Fuel quality is acceptable on the Mahendra Highway (Bhairahawa → Pokhara). **Beyond Beni, fuel is sporadic** — Beni and Jomsom are the only reliable pumps en route to Muktinath.
- ATMs: SCB, Nabil Bank, Himalayan Bank in Pokhara are the most reliable for Indian cards. **Withdrawal limits** apply — typically NPR 35,000 per transaction.
- Above Beni, ATMs are scarce. Carry physical cash.

## 8. Connectivity

- **NTC** (Nepal Telecom) — better Mustang and rural coverage.
- **Ncell** — better Pokhara and urban speeds.
- Tourist SIM at Bhairahawa with 2 photos + ID copy. ~NPR 500 for a starter pack.
- Most upmarket Pokhara resorts have fibre. Mustang region is 4G LTE (NTC) at best.

## 9. Currency

- Indian INR 100, 200, 500 notes — **accepted** in border-area shops and many places.
- Indian INR 2000 — **NOT accepted**. Don't carry.
- Best exchange: authorised counters in Bhairahawa or Pokhara Lakeside.
- Carry receipts; you may be asked at exit.

## 10. Insurance

- Indian vehicle insurance does **not** automatically cover Nepal.
- Buy a Nepal-only third-party policy at the border (cheap) OR confirm your insurer offers a Nepal rider.
- Personal travel insurance with **medical evacuation from mountainous regions** is strongly recommended given the Muktinath leg.

## 11. ACAP & Mustang

- **Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP)** — required for entering the Mustang/Annapurna region beyond Beni. SAARC nationals (Indians) NPR 1,000 per person. Issued at Pokhara (Nepal Tourism Board) or Besisahar.
- **Lower Mustang** (Jomsom, Kagbeni, Muktinath) — open zone, only ACAP required.
- **Upper Mustang** (Lo Manthang) — restricted, requires Restricted Area Permit USD 500 / 10 days. Not part of your plan.
- TIMS card — required only for trekking, not road travel.

## 12. Altitude (Muktinath = ~3,800 m)

- Acclimatise overnight at Jomsom (~2,700 m) before the Muktinath ascent.
- Diamox prophylaxis recommended — start 1 day before ascent, continue for 2 days.
- Do not drive to Muktinath the same day you arrive Pokhara. Build at least one Pokhara → Tatopani/Beni → Jomsom day.
- Symptoms to watch: headache + nausea = mild; ataxia / chest tightness / breathlessness at rest = descend immediately.

## 14. Restricted Items

- Drones — restricted; permits needed and rarely granted for private use. Leave at home.
- Satellite phones — banned without prior permit.
- Indian SIMs work in border areas (roaming) but **lose service inside Nepal** — get a Nepali SIM.
